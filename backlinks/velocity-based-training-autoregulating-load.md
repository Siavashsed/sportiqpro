## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is velocity-based training better than using percentages of your 1RM?

In my experience as a strength and conditioning coach, velocity-based training (VBT) is more useful day to day, though percentages still have a role.

The problem with percentages is that they depend on a number that goes stale. If an athlete tested a squat max eight weeks ago, that figure reflects who they were on that day. Since then they may have gained strength, lost sleep, travelled, or picked up stress from work or competition. Programming 85% of an old max assumes none of that has changed.

VBT replaces the assumption with a measurement. You track how fast the bar moves on a given load. Bar speed is tightly linked to how hard a set is, so if the bar is slower than expected in warm-ups, you lower the weight. If it's faster, you add some. The load follows the athlete's actual readiness on the day.

I saw this clearly with a rugby winger who was scheduled for 4x3 at 85% of a max he'd set two months earlier. He'd flown in from Cardiff the night before and was clearly off. His bar speed showed it immediately, so we adjusted the load rather than forcing the plan. He trained well and avoided a session that would have only dug him into a hole.

To start with VBT you need a device that measures velocity, such as a linear position transducer or a validated sensor, and a few weeks of data to build each lift's load-velocity profile. After that you set velocity targets or a velocity-loss cutoff per set.

Percentages are fine as a rough starting point, particularly for beginners. But for anyone training seriously, speed gives a better answer than an old test.
