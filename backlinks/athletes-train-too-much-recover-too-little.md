## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why is my athletic performance getting worse even though I'm training harder?

This is one of the most common problems I see as a strength and conditioning coach, and the answer is usually the same: you're not under-trained, you're under-recovered.

Training is only the stimulus. Your body actually gets fitter during recovery, when it repairs tissue, restores energy stores, and adapts to the stress you applied. If you keep stacking stimulus without giving the body time to respond, you accumulate fatigue faster than you build fitness. Performance drops, and the natural reaction is to train even more, which deepens the hole.

Here's what to check, in order:

1. Sleep. This is the biggest lever. If you're regularly getting less than 7 hours, nothing else you do will fully compensate. Keep a simple sleep log for two weeks and look at the averages honestly.

2. Training load. Look at total weekly volume across running, lifting, and any extra sessions. Many athletes don't realize how much these add up when combined.

3. Nutrition. Under-eating relative to your output limits recovery, even if you feel fine day to day.

4. Stress outside sport. School, work, and travel all draw from the same recovery budget.

5. Warning signs. Elevated resting heart rate, persistent soreness, poor mood, and flat or heavy-feeling workouts are signs you're overreaching.

The fix is usually counterintuitive: reduce volume for a week or two, protect your sleep, keep a few genuinely easy days, and let your body catch up. Most athletes bounce back faster than they expect.
