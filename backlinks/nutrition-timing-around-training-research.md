## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is the 30-minute anabolic window after a workout real, or do I need to drink a protein shake immediately after training?

Short answer: the 30-minute window is mostly a myth, and for most people it doesn't need to dictate your routine.

I'm a strength and conditioning coach, and I used to teach it as gospel. In 2019 I had a pro rugby player who rushed to his locker after every session with a shake already mixed, because I'd told him the window closed at 30 minutes. He even skipped a cool-down once to make it. When I finally let him have the shake with lunch instead, his results didn't change.

Here's what the evidence supports:

1. Daily protein total matters most. For most people who train, roughly 1.6-2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight per day is the target range.

2. Distribution helps a bit. Spreading protein across three to five meals, each with a meaningful dose, gives your muscles repeated opportunities to build and repair.

3. The post-workout window is wide. Muscle protein synthesis stays elevated for many hours after resistance training, not minutes. If you ate a reasonable meal in the few hours before training, you're already covered.

4. When timing matters more. If you train fasted, train twice in one day, or go long stretches without eating, getting protein in sooner makes more sense.

So a shake right after training isn't harmful, it's just not urgent. If it's convenient, use it. If it means skipping your cool-down or stressing about the clock, don't.

Focus first on hitting your daily numbers consistently, then sleep, then training quality. Timing is the finishing touch.
