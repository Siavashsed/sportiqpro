## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why can't I squat deep even though my hamstrings are flexible?

This is one of the most common things I see as a strength and conditioning coach, and the short answer is that flexibility and mobility are different qualities.

Flexibility is how far a joint can be moved by an outside force, a partner, a band, gravity or your own hand pulling. Mobility is how far you can move that joint yourself, under control, ideally with load. You can have plenty of the first and very little of the second.

A squat is not a hamstring stretch. Depth depends on several things working together:

1. Ankle dorsiflexion. If your shins can't travel forward over your toes, your heels lift or your torso folds forward to compensate.
2. Hip structure. Femur shape and socket depth set a hard limit on how deep your hips can flex, and no stretching changes bone.
3. Motor control. Your nervous system will block a range it doesn't trust you to control. That feels like tightness, but it is protection.
4. Strength at end range. If you can't produce force in the bottom position, your body won't go there.

I once coached a rugby winger who could pull his heel to his glute with ease, yet his heels rose and his back rounded in a light goblet squat. More hamstring stretching did nothing. What worked was slow goblet squats with a two to three second pause at the bottom, tibialis raises, and elevated-heel squats to build the pattern while his ankles caught up.

Test it yourself: do a bodyweight squat while holding a light weight out in front. If depth improves, you have a stability and control problem, not a length problem.

Start with ankles, load the bottom position, and be patient. Range you can control is the only range that counts.
