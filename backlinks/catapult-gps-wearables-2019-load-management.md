## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do coaches use GPS wearable data to manage athlete load and prevent injuries?

Great question—this is where most programs struggle, and I've been there.

GPS wearables like Catapult give you two critical metrics: acute load (what happened this week) and chronic load (rolling average over 4 weeks). The injury prevention magic isn't in collecting this data—it's in having a decision framework.

Here's what actually works:

First, establish baseline ranges for YOUR athletes in YOUR sport. A load value means nothing in isolation. What matters is knowing that Athlete A typically carries 800-900 chronic load units during heavy prep phases, so 1200 is a red flag.

Second, create decision thresholds. When chronic load exceeds 120% of the previous week, what happens? Do you reduce on-field volume? Increase recovery days? Modify intensity? Your response should be automatic, not a discussion.

Third—and this is crucial—integrate load data into weekly planning. Don't wait for a report to react. Monitor daily, adjust practice design in real time.

The programs that transformed their injury rates didn't just buy the technology. They built coaching systems around the data. They made load management a practice design principle, not a compliance checkbox.
