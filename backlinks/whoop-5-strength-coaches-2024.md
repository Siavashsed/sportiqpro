As a strength and conditioning coach who's been testing both, this depends entirely on how you're currently using wearable data.

The Whoop 5.0 launched in October 2024 with a genuinely improved blood oxygen algorithm and updated cardiovascular strain measurement. I've been running 4.0 on one of my college basketball players for eight months—pulling strain scores every Monday morning and cross-referencing them against our internal session RPE logs. That said, better hardware doesn't automatically improve coaching. I've seen teams with expensive wearables make identical periodization mistakes compared to teams without them. The upgrade makes sense if you're already: (1) using strain data alongside RPE and actual performance metrics, (2) training serious athletes where the marginal gains matter, and (3) integrated wearable data into your weekly programming reviews.

If you're still treating Whoop numbers as gospel without context—or if you're not actively programming based on recovery metrics—the 5.0 won't fix that. You'll just have prettier data to ignore.

The practical answer: upgrade if you're a high-performance environment actively using the 4.0. Skip if you're considering Whoop as your first wearable or if you're primarily a personal trainer.
