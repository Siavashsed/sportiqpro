## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which periodization model is best for building athletic performance: linear, undulating, or block periodization?

This is the wrong question-and I say that as someone who's spent 14 years trying to answer it.

I've worked with collegiate football players, professional soccer athletes, and Olympic-track competitors. Each sport, each athlete, each phase of the season demands something different. The question isn't which model is objectively best. It's which model fits your specific context.

Linear periodization works exceptionally well when athletes have consistent training time, predictable schedules, and simple movement patterns (think strength-focused training blocks). It's straightforward to program and athletes understand progressive overload intuitively.

Undulating periodization shines with athletes juggling multiple qualities-speed, strength, and power in the same week. Combat sport athletes, soccer players, and team sport athletes often thrive here because variety matches their competitive demands.

Block periodization is powerful for athletes needing deep focus on one quality before advancing to another. It's concentrated and efficient-but requires discipline and won't work if your athlete's competition schedule doesn't permit blocks of focused training.

Here's what I've learned: recovery capacity, coaching experience, athlete training age, and sport demands matter more than the model name. I've seen coaches religiously follow block periodization while overlooking that their athlete can't recover from the accumulated fatigue.
